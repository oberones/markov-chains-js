BEHIND THE LITTLE LIGHTS

Music with a one-sound memory.

+
01

A sound becomes a place

Each light is a note, a chord, or a silence with a particular length. The same notes held for a different length become a different place.

02

Listening makes the paths

The playground counts which sounds follow each other in a source. If a sound went to one neighbor three times and another once, their chances are 75% and 25%.

03

Chance takes a walk

A seeded random draw picks a path. Only the current sound matters to the next choice. That is the Markov principle: a one-sound memory, with room for surprise.

The first sound is drawn from how often sounds appeared in the source. A one-beat rest connects the end back to the beginning. Those added paths keep the walk going; they do not change starting frequencies.

Surprise and your edits reshape the learned chances. Freezing repeats a captured walk instead of making new choices. Imported overlapping notes become chord snapshots; held notes can sound again when another note changes.

Inspired by Ilana Shapiro and Mark Huber, Markov Chains for Computer Music Generation (2021). The built-in motifs and synthesizer were created for this playground.
